# CAMPAIGN CHARTER — TEMPLATE
### Tone & quest-model layer · load AFTER the master prompt, BEFORE the save state

> **What this document is.** A charter is the campaign's *tone lock* — a stable rules-of-feel layer,
> separate from both the master prompt (mechanics) and the save state (volatile state: HP, coin,
> location, quests). It exists so the DM points the master prompt's content engine at the RIGHT kind of
> beat and never manufactures escalating metaplot to fill a gap. Boot order: **master prompt → this
> charter → save state.** A charter changes rarely; the save changes every session. Keep them separate
> so a save edit can never corrupt the tone lock.
>
> **How to use this template.** Every `‹angle-bracket›` field is a slot you fill. Every line of *italic
> guidance* tells you what belongs there and is deleted once filled. Keep all proper nouns — character
> names, place names, ship/guild/house names, specific NPCs — OUT of the charter; those live in the save
> state. The charter describes a *kind* of story, not a particular cast. A filled worked example (a
> sea-voyage charter) follows the template so you can see every slot in use.

---

## THE PITCH

‹One short paragraph. The elevator pitch for the campaign's feel.›

*Guidance: Name the register (cozy / pulpy / grim / heroic / comedic), the genre touchstone if one helps
("think X"), and the **core loop** in one sentence — the repeating unit of play the campaign is built
around (a job, a delve, a court session, a heist, a leg of a journey). State what the episodes stack
INTO ("a life at sea," "a reputation in the city," "a descent through the megadungeon") and what they
explicitly are NOT ("not a conspiracy," "not a chosen-one prophecy"). No proper nouns — say "the crew,"
"the party," "the company," not their names.*

---

## HARD TONE RULES (binding on the DM)

- **‹Register› is tone and scope, NOT outcome — read this first.** The dice still rule. Karma-unlocked
  bands stay fully open; the party still meets hard combat and the occasional dark beat. The register
  names the *kind* of story (‹two or three adjectives: e.g. bounded, concrete, roguish›), never
  "nothing bad happens." ‹A worked threat example: e.g. a skeleton captain can still kill a PC.›
  Everything below constrains what kind of beat gets generated — never whether it can hurt.
- **‹Scope ceiling rule.›** *Guidance: the rule that caps how big the stakes get. State what the campaign
  refuses to escalate to. Example: "No metaplot — no apex antagonist, no shadow faction, no 'they've
  been hunting you all along.' Threats are local, immediate, resolved within the episode."*
- **‹Antagonist-class rule.›** *Guidance: define what recurring opposition IS and is not, so the DM
  doesn't promote every foe into a nemesis. Example: "Rivals are weather, not villains — they want your
  prize, not your soul. Beat them, lose them, and they're done unless YOU make an enemy worth
  remembering."*
- **‹Stakes-size rule.›** *Guidance: name the human-sized stakes this campaign trades in, and the
  cosmic stakes it forbids. Example: "Lost cargo, a sinking, a grudge, a bad debt. Not the fate of the
  world. Never the fate of a god."*
- **‹Pacing / mood rule.›** *Guidance: the felt texture moment to moment. Example: "Keep it moving and
  upbeat — salty, warm, a little roguish. Danger is real but the register is adventure, not dread."*

*Guidance: 3–5 bullets total. The first bullet (register ≠ outcome) is fixed scaffolding — keep it
always, it's the rule that stops a charter from quietly neutering the dice. The rest are yours to tune.*

## THE ‹OBJECTIVE BOARD› (the quest menu — the DM draws from THIS, not from invention)

*Guidance: Rename the board to fit the genre — Voyage Board, Contract Board, Delve Table, Docket,
Job Board, Rumor Table. This is the closed menu of episode SHAPES the campaign generates. Build a d6–d10
table. Each row is a recurring episode type plus its one-line structural shape (what the players do →
what contests it). The DM rolls or picks from this table, or skins a triggered content-roll as the
nearest row — it never invents an episode type that isn't on the board.*

Roll d‹N› or pick to fit when an objective is sought; or skin a triggered content-roll as the nearest type.

| d‹N› | ‹Episode type› | Shape |

## HOW OBJECTIVES ARE OBTAINED — THREE CHANNELS (the line between them is PLAYER AGENCY)

*Guidance: This three-channel model is the load-bearing engine of the charter and should be kept in
ALL genres — only the flavor nouns change. It ties WHO controls the episode type to the player's agency
in the fiction. Keep all three.*

**Channel A — SOUGHT (the player deliberately goes looking for work — ‹a board, a patron, a contact,
asking around›): the PLAYER chooses the episode TYPE.**
When the party deliberately seeks work, the *player picks the kind* from the board (or says "show me
what's on offer"). The DM does **not** roll *whether* it's that type. The DM rolls only to *populate*
it: which specific job of the chosen type is available, who's offering, and (via the intersection roll →
Quest Beat / Antagonist Motivation / Stakes / Wildcard) what the complication turns out to be. If the
player asks "what's on offer?", roll 2–3 types up as a menu and let them pick. Player steers the lane;
dice decorate it.

**Channel B — SPONTANEOUS (the party is NOT seeking work and the world intrudes): straight dice, no
menu.** Run the normal engine exactly as written — the per-phase Disturbance check, then the Content
roll (d100 + Karma mod → band) → intersection roll. The player does NOT choose the type; the dice do.
The charter still skins the *result* as the nearest board type / on-theme content (never an off-tone
escalation), but the player takes what the world gives. This is the cold channel that keeps the menu
honest — you can't fish for only easy jobs, because the open world rolls the full Karma-unlocked band
and can hand you a hard fight.

**Channel C — FREE ROAM (the player deliberately chooses to wander with no objective).** Distinct from
B: the party isn't passing through on the way to something — they've *chosen* to explore with no job,
just to see what's out there and let the world intrude. An intentional surrender to the dice.
Mechanically it runs the **same cold engine as Channel B** — every phase gets its Disturbance check,
full Karma-unlocked band, player takes what rolls, no menu — but free roam is the explicit context where
*being found by the world is the point*, not an interruption. Run the phase cadence normally; when
nothing triggers, that's a quiet stretch (narrate the passage; let the players ‹do downtime appropriate
to the setting›), and a triggered disturbance is the adventure the player went looking for by looking
for nothing. The player ends free roam whenever they like (set a course, take a job, make for a hub).
Free roam is how the world builds itself between objectives.

**Reward on completion (any channel):** ‹coin + occasional loot/upgrade + Reputation movement, or
whatever the campaign's reward currencies are›. Then ‹return to a hub, resupply, take the next one›.
That loop *is* the campaign.

---

## EMERGENT CONTINUITY (the only "plot" allowed — and it's player-made)

The world remembers what the party *does*. A rival they humiliated may turn up again with a grudge. A
‹place› they wronged turns cold; one they helped turns warm. A foe they beat becomes a ‹local legend /
tavern story / cautionary tale›. These are **consequences of play**, logged in the save's NPC/place
registry as they happen — never pre-authored, never a secret master plan. If the DM ever feels the urge
to reveal that several episodes were "secretly connected," DON'T. Connection only exists if the PLAYER
built it through repeated choices.

# ▼ WORKED EXAMPLE — a filled charter (sea-voyage flavor) ▼
*This is the template above with every slot filled, to show what "done" looks like. It is NOT part of a
live charter — copy the template, fill your own, and delete this whole example.*

## THE PITCH
Fun, simple, upbeat, adventurous, sea-themed. Think *Sea of Thieves*. The captain and crew run their
ship taking **voyages** — short, concrete, self-contained jobs. You sail out, you do the thing,
sometimes it goes sideways (a creature, weather, a rival crew), you come home richer (or poorer, or
wiser). Episodes stack into a life at sea, not a conspiracy.

## HARD TONE RULES
- **Upbeat is tone and scope, NOT outcome — read this first.** The dice still rule. Karma-unlocked bands
  stay fully open; the party still meets hard combat and the occasional dark beat. "Upbeat" means the
  *kind* of story (bounded, concrete, roguish), never "nothing bad happens." A skeleton captain can
  still kill a PC. Everything below constrains what kind of beat gets generated — never whether it can hurt.
- **No metaplot.** No apex antagonist, no shadow faction, no "they've been hunting you all along."
  Threats are local, immediate, resolved within the voyage.
- **Rivals are weather, not villains.** A hostile crew wants your loot or the same prize, not your soul.
  Beat them, lose them, or outsail them, and they're done — unless YOU make an enemy worth remembering.
- **Stakes stay human-sized.** Lost cargo, a sinking, a grudge, a bad debt, an empty hold. Not the fate
  of the world. Never the fate of a god.
- **Keep it moving and upbeat.** Salty, warm, a little roguish. Danger is real but the register is
  adventure, not dread.

## THE VOYAGE BOARD
| d8 | Voyage type | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Buried treasure** | A map/clue → an island → dig site → guardian or rival digging too. |
| 2 | **Bounty** | Hunt & defeat a named menace: skeleton captain, rogue beast, priced pirate. |
| 3 | **Merchant haul** | Deliver cargo intact, on time — cargo is fragile/perishable/wanted. |
| 4 | **Salvage / wreck** | Dive or board a wreck for its goods; weather, depth, or scavengers contest it. |
| 5 | **Plunder** | Raid a vessel or coastal cache for loot (Karma-relevant if it's innocents). |
| 6 | **Fetch the oddity** | Retrieve a specific object (relic, beast's egg, rare catch) guarded by X. |
| 7 | **Passenger / escort** | Carry someone or something that draws trouble; get them there alive. |
| 8 | **Rumor / explore** | An uncharted spot; go see, and the dice decide what's there. |

## THREE CHANNELS
*A — Sought:* at a port, board, or contact, the player picks the voyage type; dice populate it.
*B — Spontaneous:* at sea, not seeking work, the world intrudes — cold dice, no menu.
*C — Free roam:* the player chooses to sail open water with no job; same cold engine, being found is the point.
*Reward:* coin + occasional loot/upgrade + Ship Reputation movement → sail home, resupply, next one.

## EMERGENT CONTINUITY
A rival you humiliated returns with a grudge; a port you robbed turns cold, one you helped turns warm; a
skeleton captain you beat becomes a tavern story. Consequences of play, logged as they happen — never a
secret master plan.
